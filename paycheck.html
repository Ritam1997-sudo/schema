How often are you paid?

How often your employer issues a paycheck. This determines how your annual salary is divided, and how many paychecks you receive per year.

How are you paid?

Choose Salary for a fixed annual or per-period amount, or Hourly when your pay equals your wage rate multiplied by hours worked.

Any overtime?

Add hours worked beyond your normal schedule, typically paid at 1.5× your regular hourly rate under federal overtime rules.

Form W-4

Your W-4 instructs your employer how much federal income tax to withhold. Forms dated 2019 or earlier use allowances instead of dollar amounts.

Filing status

Your filing status determines which federal tax brackets and standard deduction apply, and affects how the multiple-jobs withholding adjustment is calculated.

Adjustments (annual $)

Dollar amounts from W-4 Steps 3 and 4 — dependents, other income, and extra deductions — that raise or lower federal withholding.

Exemptions

Certain workers qualify for exemptions from specific federal taxes. Most employees leave all of these set to No.

Pennsylvania state income tax

Pennsylvania levies a flat 3.07% income tax on wages. The rate does not vary by income level, filing status, or number of dependents.

State Unemployment Insurance (SUI)

Pennsylvania is one of the few states that requires employees to contribute to unemployment insurance — 0.07% of gross wages with no wage cap for 2026.

Earned Income Tax (local EIT)

Nearly all PA municipalities and school districts levy a local Earned Income Tax. Under Act 32, employers withhold at the higher of your home or work municipality's rate.

Local Services Tax (LST)

A flat annual tax capped at $52 in most PA municipalities, spread evenly across paychecks, funding local police, fire, and public services.

What are you paying for benefits?

Pre-tax benefits reduce your federal and FICA taxable wages. PA also excludes medical, dental, vision, FSA, and HSA from state and local taxable wages.

Retirement

Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce your federal taxable wages. Pennsylvania, however, taxes both traditional and Roth 401(k) contributions when made — the reverse of how the federal government treats them.

Post-tax deductions

Amounts taken after all taxes are calculated — such as wage garnishments, union dues, or loan repayments.

How It Works

How Pennsylvania Paycheck Withholding Works

Every PA paycheck runs through four distinct tax layers before you receive your take-home pay. Understanding each one helps employees verify their stubs are correct and helps employers set up accurate payroll from day one.

🏛️

Federal Income Tax

Calculated from the IRS's progressive bracket schedule. Your W-4 filing status and any Step 3–4 adjustments determine how much your employer withholds each pay period. A current Form W-4 keeps withholding on target all year.

🏔️

PA 3.07% Flat Tax

Pennsylvania charges a single flat rate of 3.07% on all taxable compensation — no brackets, no income thresholds. This predictability is one reason many business owners base operations in the state. Administered by the PA Department of Revenue.

📍

Local EIT & LST

Under Act 32, nearly every municipality and school district in Pennsylvania layers its own Earned Income Tax on top of state withholding, plus a flat Local Services Tax up to $52 per year. Rates vary significantly by zip code.

🛡️

FICA (Social Security & Medicare)

Employees contribute 6.2% toward Social Security (on wages up to $184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% toward Medicare, with an additional 0.9% Medicare surtax on wages above $200,000 for single filers. Employers match the base amounts dollar-for-dollar.

📋

PA Unemployment Compensation

Unlike most states, Pennsylvania requires a small employee-side contribution to unemployment insurance — currently 0.07% of gross wages with no wage cap. It is administered by the PA Department of Labor & Industry.

💼

Pre-Tax Benefits

Medical, dental, vision, FSA, and HSA premiums reduce your federal and PA state taxable wages when deducted pre-tax through a Section 125 cafeteria plan. Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce federal wages only — Pennsylvania taxes them at contribution time.

Pennsylvania's 3.07% flat rate applies to wages, salaries, net profits from self-employment, gambling winnings, and most other forms of compensation. It does not apply to Social Security benefits, most pension distributions (if contributions were previously taxed by PA), or interest and dividend income — those are taxed separately or not at all at the state level.

Using the Calculator

How to Use This PA Paycheck Calculator

Getting an accurate take-home pay estimate takes about two minutes. Here is the exact order that produces the most reliable result.

  1. Enter your earnings. Choose salary or hourly, enter your gross pay, and select your pay frequency. Bi-weekly is the most common schedule for Pennsylvania workers, but the tool handles everything from daily to annually.

  2. Complete the Federal Taxes tab. Select your W-4 version, filing status, and any Step 3–4 adjustments. If your household has two earners, toggle the "multiple jobs" option so the withholding rate reflects your combined income bracket. Reference IRS Publication 15-T if you want to check the underlying withholding tables.

  3. Set your local EIT rate. On the State & Local tab, pick your city from the dropdown or type your rate directly. If you work and live in different PA municipalities, use the higher of the two rates — that is how Act 32 works. Confirm your exact rate through the DCED register.

  4. Add benefits deductions. Pre-tax health premiums, FSA/HSA contributions, and traditional 401(k) amounts all lower your taxable wages at the federal level. The tool applies PA's different 401(k) treatment automatically.

  5. Click Calculate. The donut chart and pay stub ledger update instantly, showing gross pay, every withholding line, and your net take-home pay. Switch between "Per Paycheck" and "Annual" to see the full-year picture, or copy the summary to share with a financial advisor or employer.

Two Ways to Use This PA Tax Calculator

This take home pay calculator serves both individual workers checking their pay stubs and employers who need to estimate net payroll costs or present realistic compensation packages.

For Employees & Job Seekers

When you receive a job offer, the salary figure on paper rarely matches what hits your account. Run the gross number through this Pennsylvania paycheck calculator to see your actual bi-weekly or monthly take-home. Compare offers across Pennsylvania cities — a $70,000 role in Philadelphia carries a 3.75% local EIT, while the same salary in Lancaster is taxed at 1.10% locally. That difference amounts to over $1,400 per year in local taxes alone, enough to factor into any relocation decision.

Job seekers can also use the tool to calculate the gross salary they need to reach a target take-home. Work backward from your monthly budget — rent, loan payments, savings goals — and find the offer threshold worth accepting.

For Employers & Businesses

Pennsylvania business owners and HR teams use the PA paycheck calculator to check that gross-to-net payroll math is correct before payroll runs. Entering an employee's earnings, W-4 details, and local EIT rate produces a quick sanity check against your payroll software's output. Discrepancies often trace back to an outdated local rate, a missed Act 32 setup, or a benefits election that was not coded correctly.

During salary negotiations, showing a candidate their expected net pay — broken down by each withholding category — builds transparency and trust. Workers who understand their deductions are less likely to be surprised at their first paycheck and more likely to accept and retain their positions.

This flat structure makes the PA paycheck calculation straightforward for the state portion: multiply your taxable wages by 0.0307. The rate is set by the PA Department of Revenue and has remained at 3.07% since 2004.
  • The calculator applies your selected EIT rate to your PA-taxable wages, then adds the prorated LST. Use the municipality dropdown for a quick preset, or type your exact rate into the EIT rate field. Under Act 32, your employer should withhold at whichever rate is higher — your home municipality's or your workplace municipality's rate. If your employer is withholding at the wrong rate, contact your local tax collector to reconcile at filing time.

Pennsylvania taxes both traditional and Roth 401(k) contributions at the time they are made, unlike the federal government, which defers tax on traditional contributions until withdrawal. The upside is that your 401(k) distributions in retirement are generally not taxed by Pennsylvania. Our take home pay calculator accounts for this correctly: federal taxable wages are reduced by traditional 401(k) contributions, but PA taxable wages are not.
  • Pennsylvania is one of a small number of states that charges employees, not just employers, a share of unemployment insurance funding. For 2026, the employee Unemployment Compensation (UC) rate is 0.07% of all gross wages, with no wage base cap. On a $55,000 salary, that equals about $38.50 per year — small, but worth including in an accurate paycheck estimate. The rate is set by the PA Department of Labor & Industry.
  • Philadelphia administers its own Wage Tax independently of the Act 32 EIT system. For 2026, the resident rate is 3.75% and the non-resident rate is lower. If you live or work in Philadelphia, your employer should be withholding the Philadelphia Wage Tax rather than a standard Act 32 EIT.
